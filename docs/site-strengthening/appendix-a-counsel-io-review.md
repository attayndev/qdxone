# Appendix A — Counsel / I-O psychologist review list

Every passage below ships (or shipped) on the public site and touches
legal, scientific, or compliance-adjacent ground. Each needs sign-off from
employment counsel and/or the I/O psychologist. Verbatim quotes are from
the **revised** copy unless noted.

## For employment counsel

1. **/faq — consistency & monitoring answer (rewritten):** "Every applicant
   gets the same assessment — nobody is skipped or screened out by the
   system, and a person on your team makes every hiring decision. …
   monitoring flags if any group is being screened out at a noticeably
   lower rate than others. … No tool can promise unbiased hiring — what we
   can promise is that these practices are built in and always on."
   *Why:* the final sentence is still a promise ("built in and always on");
   confirm counsel is comfortable with it as a practices warranty.
2. **/faq — "Is it legal? Could it get me sued?"** (rewritten): "…the
   practices that fair-hiring rules generally look for." *Why:* even
   hedged, it characterizes legal standards; confirm framing.
3. **/enterprise — LL144/human-decision answer:** "…it never rejects,
   advances, or scores anyone into a decision. This human-decision design
   is directly relevant to how laws like NYC Local Law 144 define regulated
   tools…" *Why:* characterizes LL144's scope; also confirm "never …
   scores anyone into a decision" matches how counsel wants the AEDT
   question handled (we intentionally do NOT claim exemption).
4. **/enterprise — four-fifths reference:** "…monitoring flags when a
   group's selection rate falls noticeably below others (the four-fifths
   rule of thumb the EEOC uses)." *Why:* describes an EEOC standard;
   confirm description and that the product's threshold actually matches.
5. **/enterprise — EEOC Uniform Guidelines sentence:** "designed with the
   EEOC's Uniform Guidelines' expectations for job-relatedness in mind."
   *Why:* compliance-adjacent by design; the strongest allowed phrasing
   should come from counsel.
6. **/for-qsr — "A consistent process you can stand behind."** *Why:*
   replaced "fairer, more defensible" — confirm the replacement doesn't
   still imply legal defensibility to a reasonable reader.
7. **Sitewide "Score, don't filter" slogan** (shift-ready-hiring principles
   card). *Why:* it is legal positioning used as copy; counsel should bless
   the exact slogan text and its placement outside legal contexts.
8. **Trademark:** "Shift-Ready Hiring™" usage (hero + footer) — confirm ™
   claim posture and start the USPTO service-mark filing discussion
   (recommended 2026-07-14).

## For the I/O psychologist

9. **/assessments footnote:** "built on validated personality and
   motivation research and reviewed by a credentialed I/O psychologist."
   *Why:* "validated" is a term of art; confirm the exact claim they'll
   stand behind (validated constructs vs. validated instrument vs.
   criterion validity) and whether their name/credential is publishable
   `[CONFIRM]`.
10. **/assessments — "Bands, not black boxes" section:** description of
    band production and anti-gaming (attention items, response-time
    flags). *Why:* confirm the level of anti-gaming disclosure doesn't aid
    coaching, and that the description matches the scoring pipeline.
11. **Homepage — category card copy** ("the qualities that matter in
    restaurant work") and the four category names/descriptions. *Why:*
    construct naming consistency with the technical documentation.
12. **/pricing — "Benchmark against your own crew."** *Why:* incumbent
    benchmarking has adverse-impact implications; confirm the feature
    description and its aggregate-only guardrail wording.
13. **Illustrative sample items** (homepage + /assessments — three new
    items written for the site). *Why:* confirm they are safely
    non-operational and won't converge with live bank items.
