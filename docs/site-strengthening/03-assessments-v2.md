## Four categories — each one on an actual shift

**Lead-in:** Every statement feeds one of four categories — reported as
plain Low / Mid / High bands. Here&apos;s what each looks like on a shift:

- **Reliability & Drive** — Shows up Friday night even when they don&apos;t
  feel like it, and pushes to get better. The difference between a covered
  schedule and covering it yourself.
- **People Skills** — Warm with a stressed guest, easy with the crew, and
  doesn&apos;t bristle when you correct the register count.
- **Ownership** — Sees the dining room&apos;s a mess and handles it —
  nobody told them, and it wasn&apos;t &ldquo;their job.&rdquo;
- **Composure** — The line&apos;s out the door and the POS just froze. Some
  people steady; some unravel.

## Bands, not black boxes

Answers across many statements combine into a Low / Mid / High band per
category — no single item determines a band, and no single magic number
comes out. Bands are deliberately coarse: these measures aren&apos;t
precise to the decimal, and coarse bands keep small differences from being
over-read.

Can it be gamed? No assessment is game-proof, and we won&apos;t pretend
otherwise. But faking a consistent story across many statements is harder
than nailing one interview answer, and quality checks — attention items and
response-time flags — mark runs that look rushed or careless. The interview
is where you verify; the assessment tells you what to verify. It&apos;s one
input: a person makes every hiring decision, on every candidate.
