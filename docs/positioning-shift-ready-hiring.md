# QDXone positioning — Shift-Ready Hiring

Yan's canonical positioning statement (2026-07-14). This is the message every
surface should ladder up to: marketing pages, store listings, onboarding,
sales conversations, partner applications.

---

Restaurant hiring has been built around resumes, application inboxes, and
interviews. But the qualities that make someone a great hourly employee
rarely appear on a resume.

Restaurant operators need to know who will show up, work well with people,
and take ownership. Job boards can generate applicants. Traditional hiring
systems can organize them. The operator is still left trying to decide who
deserves an interview.

That is the **Applicant Volume Trap**: more applicants create more work
without necessarily creating better hires.

QDXone introduces **Shift-Ready Hiring**.

Every applicant completes a mobile application and a five-minute assessment.
QDXone evaluates the qualities that matter in restaurant work and gives the
operator a scored shortlist—so they know who to call first.

Stop collecting applicants. Start identifying the people most ready to join
the shift.

---

## Usage conventions

- **Trademark:** the category name is styled **Shift-Ready Hiring™** on the
  first/most prominent use per page (hero chip, footer tagline, page titles,
  first body mention) — running text and buttons stay clean. ™ (unregistered),
  not ® — switch only if/when a USPTO registration issues.
- **The operator mobile app is never mentioned on the marketing site.** It's
  an operator-only companion, surfaced in the dashboard after signup (Yan,
  2026-07-14).

## The load-bearing pieces

- **The named enemy:** the Applicant Volume Trap — job boards create volume,
  volume creates work, work ≠ better hires. Competitors sell more applicants
  or better organization; neither answers "who deserves an interview."
- **The category:** Shift-Ready Hiring — "shift" is the operator's own unit
  of reality; "ready" is the promise.
- **The mechanism:** mobile apply + 5-minute assessment → scored shortlist →
  "who to call first." (Consistent with the score-don't-filter product
  principle: every applicant gets assessed, nobody is silently dropped.)
- **The call to action:** "Stop collecting applicants."
